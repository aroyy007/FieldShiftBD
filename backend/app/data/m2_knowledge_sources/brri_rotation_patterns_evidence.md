# BRRI rotation pattern preview evidence

**Captured:** 2026-11-01. **Status:** provisional, source-bounded display only.

## Source

Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI), Rice-Based Farming Systems Department,
“Cropping pattern technologies,” updated 2020-06-07:

<https://brri.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dc9b933eb65569e1140d>

The displayed patterns are from the page's ecosystem (a): irrigated high to
medium-high land (level 1), clay-loam to clay soil. The provider requires saved
irrigation, land-type, and soil-type profile values to match this scope exactly.
It does not infer missing profile values.

## Patterns shown

- **BRRI pattern 1:** Boro rice followed by transplanted Aman rice.
- **BRRI pattern 2:** chickpea (BARI Chola-2), wet-seeded Aus (BR26), and
  wet-seeded Aman (BRRI dhan33).
- **BRRI pattern 4:** wet-seeded Boro (BRRI dhan28), Sesbania rostrata as green
  manure, and transplanted Aman (BR10, BR11, or BRRI dhan31).

The API displays patterns 2 and 4 as two examples beside the farmer's recorded
sequence. It does not rank the patterns. The crop/step count is derived only from
the listed sequence; it is not a measured comparison of labor, cost, or cropping
intensity.

## Limits and follow-up

The page does not supply dates, full crop calendars, local field validation,
climate-feasibility checks, water quantities, or quantified changes to soil
nutrients, carbon, erosion, yield, labor, cost, or pest pressure. A chickpea or
green-manure step is shown as a documented sequence characteristic, not proof of
a measured benefit. NASA climate evidence is not yet used to score the rotation
options. This preview cannot activate a season or authorize a crop switch.

Before broadening the catalog to other ecosystems, add a separately reviewed
source extraction and explicit match rules. Do not generalize these three
patterns to the other Bangladesh land/soil/water contexts.
