## Calendar events and provisional POWER exposure

The BR14/dhan33 pages explicitly anchor manual-weeding options to transplanting
(12–15 and 30–35 days; BR14 also 40–45 days if needed). Their irrigation text
uses observed-stage/condition triggers. Neither supplies a complete dated
booting/flowering/milk-stage sequence. These are optional/conditional reference
events, not evidence that all actions should be scheduled together.
[BRRI BR14](https://riceprofile.brri.gov.bd/varieties/25),
[BRRI dhan33](https://riceprofile.brri.gov.bd/varieties/44).

NASA's [POWER Daily API documentation](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/)
supports daily meteorological/solar time series at coordinates and explicit
start/end dates, with parameters in their source spatial resolution. It offers
UTC/LST, defaults to LST, and describes daily availability from 1981 to near-real
time. It does not provide crop stage dates or validate these calendars.

**Application inference, not a BRRI/NASA recommendation:** a read-only historical
weather summary over a source-backed calendar window is defensible as a
provisional calendar scenario, provided it names the year, selected coordinate,
date interval, method/variety, source, weather coverage/missing days, units and
time standard. A date range is not a single farmer planting date; preserve the
range or make any scenario date selection explicit. Do not call the summary a
measured field exposure, forecast, crop suitability assessment or planting
recommendation.

**Stage exposure is not established by these calendars.** Without dated stage
evidence, a documented stage model, or farmer-observed stage boundaries, show
calendar-period weather and leave stage exposure unavailable. Do not divide
total life duration into invented stages, treat transplanting as sowing, or
use NASA weather to claim agronomic approval, automatic date shifts, or a
validated yield response. An independently specified exploratory stage model
would need its own provenance and clearly labeled uncertainty; this research
does not validate one.
